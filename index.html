Spreads through the air

Tuberculosis of the lung or throat (suspected or confirmed)Airborne

PPE: N95 respirator, fit-tested

Negative pressure room free

GoPlace in the negative pressure room

Keep the door closed at all times.

  1. Place the patient in the negative pressure room. Keep the door closed. Put the Airborne sign on the door.
  2. Check the pressure monitor, or do a tissue test, to confirm negative pressure.
  3. Everyone who goes in wears a fit-tested N95.
  4. Call on-call IPC now.
  5. Keep visitors to a minimum.

Only an ordinary single room free

CautionUse this single room for now, door closed

Move to a negative pressure room as soon as one is free.

  1. Put a surgical mask on the patient. Place in this room, close the door and put the Airborne sign on it.
  2. Put a portable HEPA filter in the room. Ask facilities engineering.
  3. Everyone who goes in wears a fit-tested N95.
  4. Call on-call IPC now. Ask bed management (C.A.R.E. Unit) for a negative pressure room.
  5. If none will be free soon, arrange transfer to a hospital that has one.
  6. Keep visitors to a minimum.

No single room free

StopDo not keep the patient with other patients

Keep the patient apart until a room is free.

  1. Put a surgical mask on the patient now.
  2. Move the patient to any room with a door that closes: a single or examination room. Close the door.
  3. Put the Airborne sign on the door.
  4. No such room free? Free a single room by moving another patient out. This patient goes first.
  5. Everyone who goes in wears a fit-tested N95.
  6. Call on-call IPC now. Ask bed management (C.A.R.E. Unit) for a negative pressure room or a transfer.

When precautions end: IPC agrees before precautions end. Suspected TB: TB is no longer likely, and either another diagnosis explains the illness or 3 sputum smears are negative for AFB. Confirmed TB: at least 2 weeks of effective treatment, clinically better, and 3 sputum smears in a row negative for AFB. Collect the smears 8 to 24 hours apart, with at least one early morning sample. MDR-TB: keep for the whole admission unless IPC confirms culture conversion.

After the patient leaves: Keep the door closed and the room empty for 35 minutes at 12 or more air changes an hour, 69 minutes at 6 to 11, 104 minutes at 4 to 5, or 3.5 hours at 2 to 3 or if the rate is not known. Then do the routine clean.

Viral haemorrhagic fever

Ebola or another viral haemorrhagic fever: Marburg, Lassa, CCHF (suspected or confirmed)VHF

PPE: VHF PPE set, trained staff only

Single room with its own bathroom

GoIsolate in this room now

Only trained staff in VHF PPE go in.

  1. Close the door and put the VHF sign on it. Nobody goes in without VHF PPE.
  2. Call on-call IPC now. IPC informs public health.
  3. Log everyone who goes in.
  4. No visitors. IPC decides any exception.
  5. Use dedicated equipment, disposable where possible.
  6. Avoid aerosol-generating procedures. If one is essential, do it in a negative pressure room.

Single room without its own bathroom

CautionIsolate in this room now, with a covered commode

Only trained staff in VHF PPE go in.

  1. Close the door and put the VHF sign on it. Nobody goes in without VHF PPE.
  2. Call on-call IPC now. IPC informs public health.
  3. Use a covered bedside commode for this patient only. Bag all waste in the room.
  4. Log everyone who goes in.
  5. No visitors. IPC decides any exception.
  6. Avoid aerosol-generating procedures. If one is essential, do it in a negative pressure room.

No single room free

StopDo not keep the patient with other patients

Isolate now in any single or examination room with a door that closes.

  1. Do not touch the patient without VHF PPE. Direct the patient into any single or examination room with a door, and close it.
  2. Put the VHF sign on the door.
  3. Call on-call IPC now. IPC informs public health.
  4. Only trained staff in VHF PPE go in.
  5. Log everyone who goes in.
  6. No visitors.

When precautions end: Only IPC can end precautions, with public health. IPC and the public health authority decide case by case.

After the patient leaves: Nobody goes in or cleans until on-call IPC directs the clean and the waste.

No single room free

CautionNo single room yet: start precautions where the patient is

Move to Med Surg 3 within 4 hours.

  1. Start precautions in the current space: curtain drawn, dedicated equipment.
  2. Ask bed management to move the patient to Med Surg 3, the C. auris unit, within 4 hours. ICU, children, maternity, or Med Surg 3 full: keep a single room here and tell on-call IPC.
  3. Put the Contact sign at the bed space.
  4. Tell on-call IPC: contacts need screening.

When precautions end: Keep precautions on every admission. Do not end them, even after negative screens. Colonised and infected patients are treated the same.

After the patient leaves: Terminal clean with Oxivir or Clorox. Routine products may not kill C. auris. Hold the room until the supervisor signs off the clean, except a Med Surg 3 isolation-wing bed.

Shared bed with patients who have the same organism

GoUse this bed: cohort with the same organism

With the steps below.

  1. Check both patients: same sex and age group, and neither carries another MDRO or C. difficile that the other does not.
  2. Keep the beds more than 1 m apart and the curtain drawn.
  3. Put the Contact sign on the door.
  4. Give each patient their own equipment.
  5. Tell IPC about the cohort.

Shared bed with patients who do not have it

CautionOnly if the roommate has none of these risks

Any of them: do not use the bed.

  1. The roommate must not have: a central line, urinary catheter, ventilator or drain; an open wound; immunocompromise; a long expected stay; broad-spectrum antibiotics; or surgery planned.
  2. Any of these? Do not use the bed. Ask bed management (C.A.R.E. Unit) for a single room.
  3. Keep the beds more than 1 m apart and the curtain drawn.
  4. The patient uses their own toilet or a dedicated commode.
  5. Put the Contact sign at the bed space.
  6. Same sex and age group (adult or child) in one room.

  3. Put the Contact sign at the bed space.

When precautions end: Only IPC can end precautions. 3 negative screens of the positive sites, taken 3 days apart. Off antibiotics for at least 3 days.
